\chapter{Conclusion}

\quad This thesis investigates a decentralized MARL framework to address GW load balancing in LEO NTN. By modeling the FLSO problem as a MMDP, This thesis developes a multi-agent dueling DDQN algorithm designed to optimize network-wide utility under rapidly time-varying LEO geometries. Extensive simulations demonstrate that the proposed policy achieves the most robust performance trade-off compared to heuristics and standard RL baselines. While signal-based schemes provided high link quality at the cost of excessive switching instability, and distance-based heuristics stabilized connections but led to severe load imbalance, the proposed FLSO successfully balanced these conflicting objectives. It suppressed UFLSOs to levels comparable to conservative geometric strategies while preserving link quality similar to signal-based methods. Crucially, the proposed algorithm significantly improved Jain’s fairness index and total throughput, thereby unlocking latent capacity in the ground segment through the intelligent use of multi-GW connectivity. Consequently, this thesis confirms that a lightweight decentralized MARL-based controller can deliver a favorable balance among switching stability, backhaul link quality, and network efficiency.
