\chapter{Conclusion} % top level followed by section, subsection
% \quad This thesis presents an OID-based CHO strategy designed to address mobility issues that still occur in distance-based CHO in LEO satellite EMC environments. Rapid satellite movement in EMC environments often leads to frequent HO, which causes issues such as UHO and RLF. The proposed OID-based CHO extends the existing distance-based strategy by newly defining the concept of distance from the orbit line, enabling more appropriate cell selection according to UE conditions. This allows the UE to remain connected to the most optimal satellite cell, providing a more stable communication environment even under rapid satellite movement. Simulation results show that OID-based CHO provides significant improvements over existing signal strength-based and distance-based strategies, including reduced RLF and UHO occurrence rates, and reduced RB usages. In addition, this strategy also effectively compromises the trade-off relationship between reduced UHO occurrence rates and reduced DL SINR in existing strategies by adjusting the offset, thereby achieving optimal results. Furthermore, the proposed approach reduces UE complexity and resolves practical challenges in parameter delivery by using a common offset for all UEs instead of assigning individual offsets.
