\section{Simulation setup}
\quad In this section, we evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithm alongside DQN-based, distance-based, and signal-based FLSO algorithms through extensive simulations. The simulation environment considers the actual deployment of GWs and satellites; specifically, it is based on operational SpaceX Starlink GW sites in the United States and models the partially approved Gen2 satellite constellation by the FCC []. The orbit of the agent satellite is propagated using TLE data for Starlink satellite No.~1008, retrieved on December 5, 2024. As the satellite passes over the GWs, it periodically measures the RSRP.
% 본 섹션에서는 제안된 알고리즘의 성능을 DQN 기반, 거리 기반, 신호 기반 FLSO 알고리즘과 함께 광범위한 시뮬레이션을 통해 평가합니다. 시뮬레이션 환경은 실제 GW 및 위성 배치를 고려합니다. 구체적으로, 미국 내 운영 중인 SpaceX Starlink GW 사이트를 기반으로 하며 FCC로부터 부분 승인된 2세대 위성군을 모델링합니다. 에이전트 위성의 궤도는 2024년 12월 5일에 수신된 Starlink 위성 No. 1008의 TLE 데이터를 사용하여 전파됩니다. 위성이 GW 상공을 지날 때 주기적으로 RSRP를 측정합니다.

GW load for baseline characterization is obtained by running the distance-based selection over the deployed constellation snapshot and counting attached satellites at each time step. Consequently, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:0.complexity}, we visualize the normalized number of satellites attached per gateway under random satellite placements, which highlights the inherent spatial skewness and motivates load-aware FLSO design.

The compared FLSO schemes are defined as follows.
% 비교된 FLSO 방식은 다음과 같이 정의됩니다.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DQN-based FLSO}: The DQN baseline is trained using the same state, action, and reward definitions as the proposed dueling DQN agent, but with a conventional single-stream Q-network (no dueling architecture). Learning uses temporal-difference updates with experience replay and a target network. After training, the learned Q-network is frozen and evaluated without further exploration.
    % DQN 기반 FLSO: DQN 기준 모델은 제안된 듀얼링 DQN 에이전트와 동일한 상태, 행동, 보상 정의를 사용하여 훈련되지만, 일반적인 단일 스트림 Q-네트워크(듀얼링 아키텍처 없음)를 사용합니다. 학습은 경험 리플레이와 타겟 네트워크를 이용한 시간차 업데이트를 사용합니다. 훈련 후, 학습된 Q-네트워크는 고정되어 더 이상의 탐색 없이 평가됩니다.
    
    \item \textbf{Distance-based FLSO}: This heuristic selects the GW with the minimum geometric distance to the satellite. A FLSO event is triggered when the distance to a target GW becomes shorter than that to the serving GW by at least a predefined gap $\Delta d_{\mathrm{th}}$. This rule can be interpreted as a special case of the 3GPP dual-threshold distance-based framework (Events D1/D2 in TS~38.331~\cite{rel_38331}), where the thresholds and hysteresis are chosen such that the entering condition reduces to a distance-gap constraint between serving and candidate gateways. It thus provides a standard-aligned, but purely geometry-driven, feeder-link selection baseline.
    % 거리 기반 FLSO: 이 휴리스틱은 위성에 가장 가까운 기하학적 거리를 가진 GW를 선택합니다. FLSO 이벤트는 대상 GW까지의 거리가 서빙 GW까지의 거리보다 미리 정의된 간격 $\Delta d_{\mathrm{th}}$ 이상으로 짧아질 때 트리거됩니다. 이 규칙은 3GPP 이중 임계값 거리 기반 프레임워크(TS 38.331의 이벤트 D1/D2)의 특별한 경우로 해석될 수 있으며, 여기서 임계값과 히스테리시스는 진입 조건이 서빙 게이트웨이와 후보 게이트웨이 간의 거리-간격 제약으로 축소되도록 선택됩니다. 따라서 이는 표준에 부합하지만 순전히 기하학적으로 구동되는 피더 링크 선택 기준선을 제공합니다.
    
    \item \textbf{Signal-based FLSO}: This scheme adapts the conventional A3 event-based HO rule to feeder-link selection. A FLSO event is triggered when the RSRP of a target GW exceeds that of the serving GW by more than a configured offset. Different offsets realize different hysteresis levels. While this approach is compatible with existing NR measurement procedures, it relies solely on instantaneous or filtered signal strength and does not exploit geometric predictability or GW load information.
    % 신호 기반 FLSO: 이 방식은 기존의 A3 이벤트 기반 HO 규칙을 피더 링크 선택에 적용합니다. FLSO 이벤트는 대상 GW의 RSRP가 서빙 GW의 RSRP를 구성된 오프셋보다 초과할 때 트리거됩니다. 다른 오프셋은 다른 히스테리시스 수준을 실현합니다. 이 접근 방식은 기존 NR 측정 절차와 호환되지만, 순간적이거나 필터링된 신호 강도에만 의존하며 기하학적 예측 가능성이나 GW 부하 정보를 활용하지 않습니다.
    
    \item \textbf{Proposed FLSO}: The proposed dueling DQN-based policy builds upon the D2-type distance-based concept by incorporating ephemeris-aware geometry, L3-filtered RSRP, gateway load indicators, and switching penalties jointly into the state and reward. In contrast to the distance- and signal-only baselines, it learns a gateway selection strategy that is simultaneously distance-aware, link-quality-aware, and load-aware, while remaining consistent with the standardized measurement abstractions.
    % 제안된 FLSO: 제안된 듀얼링 DQN 기반 정책은 천체력 인지 기하학, L3 필터링된 RSRP, 게이트웨이 부하 지표 및 스위칭 페널티를 상태와 보상에 공동으로 통합하여 D2 유형 거리 기반 개념을 기반으로 합니다. 거리 및 신호 전용 기준선과 대조적으로, 표준화된 측정 추상화와 일관성을 유지하면서 거리, 링크 품질 및 부하를 동시에 인지하는 게이트웨이 선택 전략을 학습합니다.
\end{itemize}



\section{Key Performance Indicators}
\quad We evaluate five key performance indicators that together capture FLSO stability, link quality, and GW load balancing. Unless otherwise noted, all metrics are computed per episode and averaged over time and satellites.

FLSO measures how many times, on average, each satellite changes its serving GW during an episode. It reflects the overall switching activity of the FLSO policy; smaller values indicate a more stable association with fewer disruptions and reduced control overhead.

UFLSO refines this by counting switchovers that are followed by a very short dwell time, shorter than a predefined minimum time-of-stay. Such events correspond to ping-pong or impulsive decisions that do not yield lasting performance gains. This KPI quantifies how many switches are effectively wasteful; a desirable policy minimizes both the total number of switchovers and the fraction that are unnecessary.

ToS denotes how long a satellite continuously remains connected to the same GW between two consecutive switchovers. We report the average dwell time over all satellites and all stays. Larger ToS values indicate that the policy maintains more persistent feeder-link associations, reducing signaling overhead and limiting service interruptions while still allowing beneficial handovers.

RSRP evaluates the long-term quality of the selected feeder links. At each time step, we observe the L3-filtered RSRP of the GW actually serving each satellite, using the standardized IIR-based parameters in Table~\ref{tab:sim_params}. We then average this serving-link RSRP across all satellites and time. Higher values mean that satellites are more consistently attached to gateways providing strong received power, showing that stability is not obtained at the cost of degraded link quality.

GW load characterizes how evenly the feeder-link load is distributed across gateways. For each GW, we monitor how many satellites are attached over time, summarize this as its average load, and track the maximum load observed across all GWs and time. Lower peak load and a tighter spread of average loads indicate more balanced utilization of feeder-link resources and a reduced risk of persistent GW bottlenecks.



\section{Results and Discussion}
\quad Fig.~\ref{fig:reward} illustrates the normalized episodic reward during training for the proposed FLSO agent and the DQN baseline. Both agents rapidly improve in the early stage and exceed a normalized reward of approximately 0.9 within several thousand episodes, indicating successful learning of feasible FLSO policies under the considered environment. However, the proposed agent converges slightly faster and attains a consistently higher asymptotic reward. The zoomed view highlights that the proposed scheme exhibits significantly lower variance and avoids the deep reward drops frequently observed with the DQN. This behavior confirms that the architectural and objective design of the proposed agent stabilizes value estimation and produces a more reliable policy, while the baseline DQN remains sensitive to non-stationarity and occasionally explores suboptimal switching patterns even in late training.

Fig.~\ref{fig:1.flso} compares the average number of FLSOs for the proposed FLSO scheme against the DQN baseline, distance-based, and signal-based heuristics. The proposed method achieves a substantially lower switching frequency than the DQN baseline, reducing the average FLSO count by roughly one quarter while also exhibiting smaller variability across episodes. This shows that a conventional value-based agent without explicit load- and stability-aware design leads to unstable gateway selection, whereas the proposed formulation yields more consistent decisions.

For the distance-based heuristic, the FLSO count is highly sensitive to the distance gap $\Delta d_{\mathrm{th}}$. Small thresholds (0--20 km) result in persistently higher switching rates than the proposed method, as satellites aggressively move to marginally closer gateways despite limited gain. The signal-based A3-type scheme exhibits the most pronounced trade-off. Low offsets (0 and 2 dB) trigger excessive switchovers, saturating the FLSO metric and clearly demonstrating the ping-pong tendency of purely RSRP-driven decisions. Larger offsets (4--8 dB) progressively suppress switchovers and can yield fewer FLSOs than the proposed method at the highest offset; however, such aggressive hysteresis forces satellites to remain attached to suboptimal gateways for extended periods, which, as confirmed by other KPIs, degrades link quality and load-balancing performance. Overall, the results confirm that the proposed FLSO policy provides a favorable balance between minimizing unnecessary switchovers and avoiding overly conservative behavior that harms backhaul efficiency.

Fig.~\ref{fig:4.rsrp} shows the distribution of the average serving-link RSRP. The proposed scheme achieves RSRP levels comparable to, or slightly higher than, those of the DQN and tuned distance-based baselines, while maintaining a significantly lower FLSO count. These results indicate that the proposed method operates close to the Pareto frontier between link-quality maximization and switching overhead, whereas heuristic hysteresis tuning either overreacts or underreacts to channel variations.

Fig.~\ref{fig:5.complexity} investigates gateway load in terms of the average number of attached satellites per GW. While the global mean is fixed by the constellation size, the spread of these distributions reveals the effectiveness of load balancing. The proposed method exhibits the tightest box and shortest whiskers, indicating that satellites are distributed across gateways in a highly uniform manner and that no gateway persistently attracts a disproportionate share of the load. The DQN baseline shows a wider spread, implying less regularized gateway selection. Distance-based methods gradually worsen the load distribution as $\Delta d_{\mathrm{th}}$ increases: larger thresholds promote overly sticky associations to nearby gateways and amplify spatial skewness. Signal-based schemes display substantial variability across offsets; low offsets lead to volatile associations, whereas high offsets lock satellites to a subset of gateways. Taken together with the RSRP and (U)FLSO results, these observations demonstrate that the proposed FLSO framework---built to be consistent with standardized distance-based events yet augmented with load-awareness---jointly optimizes switching stability, feeder-link quality, and gateway load balancing in a way that heuristic and standard DRL baselines cannot match.
