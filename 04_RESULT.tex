\section{Simulation setup}
\quad In this section, we evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithm alongside DQN-based FLSO, distance-based and signal-based FLSO algorithms through extensive simulations. The simulation environment considers the actual deployment of gateways and satellites; specifically, it is based on operational SpaceX Starlink gateway sites in the United States and models the partially approved Gen2 satellite constellation by the FCC. The orbit of the agent satellite is propagated using TLE data for Starlink satellite No. 1008, retrieved on December 5, 2024. As the satellite passes over the GWs, it periodically measures the RSRP. 
GW load for baseline characterization is obtained by running the distance-based selection over the deployed constellation snapshot and counting attached satellites at each time step. % 게이트웨이 부하는 배치된 성좌 스냅샷에서 거리 기반 선택 알고리즘을 수행하며 시간별 GW에 연결된 위성 수를 집계하여 산출.

\begin{itemize}
\item DQN-based FLSO: It is trained using the identical state, action, and reward as defined for the dueling DQN. Both algorithms are initially trained the subsequent simulations are conducted using the learned Q-networks. Learning proceeds via temporal difference updates with experience replay and periodic target network synchronization without dueling network.

\item Distance-based FLSO: This algorithm selects the GW based on the minimum distance between the LEO satellite and GWs. A FLSO event is triggered when the distance to a target GW is shorter than that to the serving GW by a predefined threshold, denoted by \( \Delta d_{\mathrm{th}} \).

\item Signal-Based FLSO: It is implemented by adapting the conventional HO mechanism. Specifically, we employ the widely used A3 event-based FLSO algorithm as a baseline. In this algorithm, a FLSO event is triggered when the difference between the RSRP of the serving GW and that of target GWs exceeds a predefined threshold, denoted by \( offset \).
\end{itemize}
  



\section{Key Performance Indicators}
\quad We evaluate five key performance indicators that together capture feeder-link switching stability, link quality, and gateway load balancing. Unless otherwise noted, all metrics are computed per episode and averaged over time and satellites.
FLSO measures how many times, on average, each satellite changes its serving gateway during an episode. It reflects the overall switching activity of the FLSO policy. A smaller value indicates a more stable association with fewer disruptions and reduced control overhead.
UFLSO refines this by counting switchovers that are followed by a very short dwell time, shorter than a predefined minimum time-of-stay (set to 1 s in our configuration). Such events correspond to ping–pong or impulsive decisions that do not yield lasting performance gains. This KPI quantifies how many switches are effectively wasteful, and a desirable policy minimizes both the total number of switchovers and the fraction that are unnecessary.
ToS denotes how long a satellite continuously remains connected to the same gateway between two consecutive switchovers. We report the average dwell time over all satellites and all stays. Larger ToS values indicate that the policy maintains more persistent feeder-link associations, which reduces signaling overhead and limits service interruptions while still allowing handovers when they are genuinely beneficial.
RSRP evaluates the long-term quality of the selected feeder links. At each time step, we observe the L3-filtered RSRP of the gateway actually serving each satellite, using the standardized IIR-based filtering parameters defined in Table~\ref{tab:sim_params}. We then average this serving-link RSRP across all satellites and time. Higher values mean that satellites are more consistently attached to gateways providing strong received power, indicating that the policy improves link quality rather than achieving stability at the expense of performance.
GW load characterizes how evenly the feeder-link load is distributed across gateways. For each gateway, we monitor how many satellites are attached over time, summarize this as its average load, and also track the maximum load observed across all gateways and time. Lower peak load and a tighter spread of average loads across gateways indicate more balanced utilization of feeder-link resources and a reduced risk of gateway congestion or bottlenecks.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\quad Fig.~\ref{fig:1.flso} compares the average number of FLSO for the proposed FLSO scheme against the DQN baseline, distance-based, and signal-based heuristics. The proposed method achieves a substantially lower switching frequency than the DQN baseline, reducing the average FLSO count by roughly one quarter while also exhibiting a smaller variability across episodes. This indicates that simply applying a conventional value-based agent without explicit load-aware design leads to unstable gateway selection, whereas the proposed formulation yields more consistent decisions.

For the distance-based heuristic, the FLSO count is highly sensitive to the distance threshold. Small thresholds (0--20 km) result in persistently higher switching rates than the proposed method, as satellites aggressively move to marginally closer gateways. Increasing the threshold (30--50 km) alleviates this effect and brings the FLSO count close to that of the proposed scheme, but only at the cost of relying on a hand-tuned parameter that does not account for gateway load or link quality beyond geometry.

The signal-based A3-type scheme exhibits the most pronounced trade-off. Low offsets (0 and 2 dB) trigger excessive switchovers, saturating the FLSO metric and clearly demonstrating the ping–pong tendency of purely RSRP-driven decisions. Larger offsets (4--8 dB) progressively suppress switchovers and can attain fewer FLSOs than the proposed method at the highest offset; however, such aggressive hysteresis inevitably forces satellites to remain attached to suboptimal gateways for extended periods, which, as shown in other KPIs, degrades link quality and load balancing performance. Overall, the results confirm that the proposed FLSO policy provides a favorable balance between minimizing unnecessary switchovers and avoiding overly conservative behavior that harms backhaul efficiency.

Fig.~\ref{fig:2.ufso} evaluates the number of unnecessary feeder-link switchovers (UFLSO). The proposed scheme and all distance-based variants effectively suppress UFLSO events, indicating that their FLSOs are mostly persistent and justified. The DQN baseline exhibits a small but non-negligible number of unnecessary switches, reflecting instability in its learned policy. The signal-based scheme with low offsets is clearly problematic: it generates a large number of short-lived switchovers, which directly manifests the ping-pong behavior implied by its high FLSO count. Increasing the offset mitigates UFLSO but only by making the policy overly sticky. Overall, the proposed method uniquely combines low FLSO and near-zero UFLSO, achieving stable adaptations without ad hoc parameter choices.

Fig.~\ref{fig:3.tos} compares the average ToS. Excluding the aggressively reactive signal-based cases (0-2 dB), all schemes yield relatively long ToS due to the minimum dwell-time constraint. Nonetheless, the proposed method consistently attains the longest or near-longest ToS with tight episode-wise variation, confirming that its switchovers are both infrequent and durable. The DQN baseline and distance-based heuristics provide slightly shorter ToS or larger spread, indicating residual oscillations or sensitivity to the chosen distance thresholds. Low-offset signal-based schemes suffer from significantly reduced ToS, aligned with their high UFLSO, while large offsets recover long ToS at the cost of degraded RSRP and load balancing. These trends reinforce that the proposed policy realizes stable gateway associations without sacrificing responsiveness.

Fig.~\ref{fig:4.rsrp} shows the distribution of the average serving-link RSRP. The proposed scheme achieves RSRP levels comparable to, or slightly higher than, those of the DQN and optimized distance-based baselines, while maintaining a significantly lower FLSO count. In contrast, the signal-based scheme with small A3 offsets (0–2 dB) attains marginally higher RSRP at the expense of extremely frequent switchovers, which is undesirable in practice. As the offset increases (4–8 dB), the signal-based scheme becomes overly conservative: satellites remain attached to outdated gateways, and the average RSRP degrades sharply. These results confirm that the proposed method operates close to the Pareto frontier between link-quality maximization and switching overhead, whereas heuristic hysteresis tuning either overreacts or underreacts to channel variations.

Fig.~\ref{fig:5.complexity} investigates gateway load in terms of the average number of attached satellites per GW. While the global mean is fixed by the constellation size, the spread of these distributions reveals the effectiveness of load balancing. The proposed method exhibits the tightest box and shortest whiskers, indicating that satellites are distributed across gateways in a highly uniform manner and that no gateway persistently attracts a disproportionate share of the load. The DQN baseline shows a wider spread, implying less regularized gateway selection. Distance-based methods gradually worsen the load distribution as the distance threshold increases: larger thresholds promote “sticky” associations to geographically nearby gateways, thereby amplifying spatial skewness. Signal-based schemes display substantial variability across offsets; low offsets lead to volatile associations, while high offsets lock satellites to a subset of gateways, in both cases degrading load fairness. Taken together with the RSRP and (U)FLSO results, these observations demonstrate that the proposed FLSO framework jointly optimizes switching stability, feeder-link quality, and gateway load balancing in a way that heuristic and standard DRL baselines cannot match.
