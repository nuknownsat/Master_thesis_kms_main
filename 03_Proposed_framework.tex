\section{System model}
\quad This thesis considers a large-scale LEO satellite constellation deployed across $P$ orbital planes, represented by the set $\mathcal{P} = \left\{p \mid p = 1, \dots, P\right\}$. The constellation consists of $N$ LEO satellites in total, distributed across these planes, with $N_p$ satellites in plane $p$. We write the overall satellite index set as $\mathcal{S} = \{ s_{p,i} \mid p = 1, \dots, P;\ i = 1, \dots, N_p \}$, and its cardinality as $\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert = \sum_{p=1}^{P} N_p$. For simplicity in the following equations, we denote a single satellite by its global index $n \in \{1, \dots, N\}$. The LEO satellite $n$ connects to ground GWs via feeder link, where the set of GWs is denoted as $\mathcal{G} = \left\{m \mid m = 1, 2, \dots, M\right\}$. The coordinates of each LEO satellite $n$ in the ECEF coordinate system at a given time slot $t$, where $t \in \{0, 1, \dots, T\}$, are given by $l_n(t) = (x_n(t), y_n(t), z_n(t))$.

The primary challenge addressed in this thesis is GW load balancing. We define the load of a GW \( m \) at time \( t \), denoted as $\mathcal{L}_m(t)$, as the set of satellites $n \in \mathcal{S}$ currently connected to it. The instantaneous load is $\lvert \mathcal{L}_m(t) \rvert$.

The LEO satellite \( n \) evaluates the RSRP from signals transmitted by the GW \( m \) at periodic interval \( T_m \). The RSRP \( R_{m,n} \) is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
R_{m,n} = EIRP_{m} + G_{n} - PL_{m,n} - 10 \log_{10}(q_{RB}),
\label{eq:rsrp}
\end{equation}
where \( EIRP_{m} \) is the EIRP of the GW \( m \), defined as \( EIRP_{m} = P_{m} + G_{m} \), with \( P_{m} \) being the transmit power and \( G_{m} \) the transmit antenna gain of the GW $m$ towards the LEO satellite \( n \). \( G_{n} \) represents the receive antenna gain of the LEO satellite \( n \). The term \( 10 \log_{10}(q_{RB}) \) is used to normalize the RSRP measurement, accounting for a single resource block out of the total resource blocks \( q_{RB} \) allocated to the link. $PL_{m,n}$ is the channel model between the GW \( m \) and the LEO satellite \( n \) is provided in the 3GPP documentation \cite{3gppchanel} and is calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
&PL_{m,n} = P_{{LOS,{m,n}}} ( FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m) + SF_{m,n} )\notag\\
&+ P_{{NLOS,{m,n}}} ( FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m) + SF_{m,n} + CL_{m,n} ),
\end{align}
$P_{{LOS,{m,n}}}$ and $P_{{NLOS,{m,n}}}$ denote the probabilities that the LEO satellite \( n \) to GW \( m \) is in a LOS and NLOS condition, respectively. $FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m)$ denotes the FSPL as a function of the distance $d_{m,n}$ between the LEO satellite \( n \) and the GW \( m \) and the frequency $f_m$ of the GW \( m \) uplink feeder link frequency. $SF_{m,n}$ represents the shadow fading, which accounts for the signal attenuation caused by obstacles such as buildings, trees, or terrain variations in the propagation path. $CL_{m,n}$ denotes the clutter loss, reflecting additional signal degradation due to environmental clutter. Specifically, the $FSPL_{m,n}$ is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
FSPL_{m,n} = 32.45 + 20log_{10}(f_m) + 20log_{10}(d_{m,n}).
\end{equation}

At each measurement instant $t$, the LEO satellite \( n \) samples the RSRP from GW \( m \) and all neighboring GWs within instantaneous visibility region, yielding the raw RSRP $R_{m,n}(t)$. To mitigate the effects of shadow fading and measurement noise, these raw RSRP $R_{m,n}(t)$ samples are processed at the RRC layer using a L3 IIR filter \cite{3gppl3}. Denoting the filtered RSRP by $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$, the update equation is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t) = a{R}_{m,n}(t) + (1 - a)\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t),
  \label{eq:L3filter_new}
\end{equation}
where $a \in (0,1)$ is the L3 forgetting factor, defined as $a = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{K/4}$, and $K$ is the filter coefficient. The initial condition is set as $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(0) = R_{m,n}(0)$. Finally, the LEO satellite compiles a measurement report containing the L3-filtered RSRP $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$ of the serving and neighboring GWs and transmits this report back to the serving GW \( m \) over the feeder link.

\section{Problem formulation}
\quad In this section, we address the optimization problem for the LEO satellite constellation. The objective is to find an optimal FLSO policy $\pi$ that dynamically assigns each satellite $n \in \mathcal{S}$ to a GW $m \in \mathcal{G}$ at each time slot $t$.

The primary goal is to minimize the overall network load imbalance while simultaneously maximizing the collective link quality RSRP and minimizing the instability caused by frequent FLSOs. We define $m_t^{(n)}$ as the GW selected by satellite $n$ at time $t$. This problem can be formulated as a multi-objective optimization problem.

We define the following cost and reward components for each satellite $n$ at time $t$:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FLSO Cost:} A cost $C_{FS}$ is incurred if the satellite switches its serving GW, penalizing instability.
    \begin{equation}
    FS_{n}(t) = \begin{cases} C_{FS} & \text{if } m_t^{(n)} \neq m_{t-1}^{(n)} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
    \label{eq:flso_cost}
    \end{equation}
    
    \item \textbf{Load Imbalance Cost:} A cost is incurred for connecting to a congested GW. We use a convex function (i.e., quadratic) of the normalized load $\lambda_m(t)$ to strongly penalize highly congested GWs. This cost is central to our problem.
    \begin{equation}
    L_{n}(t) = (\lambda_{m_t^{(n)}}(t))^2
    \label{eq:load_cost}
    \end{equation}
    
    \item \textbf{Link Quality Reward:} A reward is gained based on the L3-filtered RSRP from the selected GW.
    \begin{equation}
    R_{n}(t) = \widetilde{R}_{m_t^{(n)},n}(t)
    \label{eq:rsrp_reward}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

The global optimization problem \textbf{P1} is to find the policy $\pi$ that minimizes the total expected cumulative cost (and maximizes rewards) for all $N$ satellites over the time horizon $T$.

\begin{align}
& \textbf{P1:}\quad \operatorname*{\min}_{\pi} \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{t=0}^{T} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \gamma^t \Big( w_{FS} FS_{n}(t) + w_{L} L_{n}(t) - w_{R} R_{n}(t) \Big) \right]\\
& \quad \text{s.t.} \nonumber \\
& \quad \textbf{C1 :}\quad \theta_{m,n}(t) \geq \theta_{\text {min}}, \quad \forall{n}, \forall{m} \in \mathcal{G}_t^{(n)}, \forall{t},\\
& \quad \textbf{C2 :}\quad \widetilde R_{m,n}(t) \geq R_{\text {min}}, \quad \forall{n}, \forall{m} \in \mathcal{G}_t^{(n)}, \forall{t},\\
& \quad \textbf{C3 :}\quad m_t^{(n)} \in \mathcal{G}_t^{(n)}, \quad \forall{n}, \forall{t}.
\end{align}

In \textbf{P1}, $w_{FS}$, $w_{L}$, and $w_{R}$ are the weighting factors that balance the trade-off between switching stability, load balancing, and link quality. $\gamma \in [0, 1]$ is the discount factor for future costs.

The constraints define the set of available GWs, $\mathcal{G}_t^{(n)}$, for satellite $n$ at time $t$.
Constraint \textbf{C1} ensures the elevation angle $\theta_{m,n}(t)$ to an available GW $m$ exceeds the minimum threshold $\theta_{\min}$, guaranteeing a reliable line-of-sight.
Constraint \textbf{C2} ensures the filtered RSRP $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$ from an available GW exceeds the minimum required signal quality $R_{\min}$.
Constraint \textbf{C3} mandates that each satellite $n$ must select a GW $m_t^{(n)}$ from the set of currently available GWs $\mathcal{G}_t^{(n)}$ that satisfy C1 and C2.

This problem is characterized by high-dimensional state spaces and complex, time-varying dynamics, making it NP-hard. We therefore model this problem as a MDP to find an optimal FLSO policy using reinforcement learning techniques.

\section{Proposed algorithm}
\quad To address the problem \textbf{P1}, we transform it into a dynamic programming framework to derive an optimal FLSO policy. We model the FLSO optimization problem as an MDP due to its ability to handle sequential decision-making in dynamic environments.


\subsection{MDP modeling}
\quad The LEO satellite's state \(s_t\) evolves over time and actions \(a_t\) impact future reward \(r_t\) with discount factor \(\gamma\) \cite{intro_RL}. This framework enables the LEO satellite to learn an optimal FLSO policy through reinforcement learning, effectively balancing link quality against network load. The state space \(s_t\) of the MDP is calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
s_t &= \left\{ l_n(t), m_{t-1}, \vec{\widetilde{R}}_n(t), \vec{\lambda}(t) \right\}.
\end{align}
The state is defined as a tuple comprising the LEO satellite's ECEF coordinates \(l_n(t)\), the ID of the previously connected GW \( m_{t-1} \), the vector of L3-filtered RSRPs from all visible GWs $\vec{\widetilde{R}}_n(t) = [\widetilde{R}_{1,n}(t), \dots, \widetilde{R}_{M,n}(t)]$, and the vector of normalized loads for all GWs $\vec{\lambda}(t) = [\lambda_1(t), \dots, \lambda_M(t)]$. This state provides the agent with full visibility of the network's quality and congestion status.

The action space \( {a_t} \) involves the LEO satellite $n$ selecting the optimal GW $m \in \mathcal{G}$ at each step. In line with constraints \textbf{C1-C3}, the action space is the set of available GWs that meet the minimum elevation and RSRP thresholds:
\begin{align}
{a_t} &= \{ m_{t} | m_{t} \in \mathcal{G}_{t}^{(n)} \}.
\end{align}
The action \(a_t\) is the selection of the next GW, $m_t$, from the set of available GWs \( \mathcal{G}_{t}^{(n)} \).

The immediate reward \(r_{t}\) is formulated to directly optimize the objective function defined in \textbf{P1}. It penalizes FLSO costs and high-load connections while rewarding high-quality RSRP links. It is calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
r_t &= w_{R} R_{n}(t) - w_{FS} FS_{n}(t) - w_{L} L_{n}(t),
\end{align}
where $FS_n(t)$, $L_n(t)$, and $R_n(t)$ are the costs and rewards defined in Eq. \eqref{eq:flso_cost}, \eqref{eq:load_cost}, and \eqref{eq:rsrp_reward}, and $w_{FS}, w_{L}, w_{R}$ are the corresponding weights from \textbf{P1}.

Discount factor $\gamma$ is a value between 0 and 1, which represents the rate at which future rewards are discounted when considering them at the current step.

\label{Proposed Dueling DDQN Algorithm}
\quad In this paper, we employ a reinforcement learning algorithm to enable LEO satellites to operate in an dynamic environment. As depicted in Fig. 2, the architecture of the proposed dueling DDQN algorithm, incorporating a \(\epsilon\)-greedy policy, is designed to optimize FLSO in LEO satellite networks. The LEO satellite receives feedback to execute actions that sustain both a prolonged ToS and an elevated RSRP, while minimizing the frequency of FLSO and, critically, gateway congestion. We enhance the traditional DQN framework by incorporating DDQN, PER, and dueling DQN to improve stability, learning efficiency, and robustness against noise in LEO satellite communication systems.

The DQN algorithm \cite{mnih2013playing} approximates the Q-function \( Q(s_t, a_t; \theta) \), which maps a state \( s_t \) and action \( a_t \) to an expected cumulative reward, using a DNN parameterized by \( \theta \). Two DNNs are utilized: an online network for action selection and a target network for value estimation. The Q-function is trained to minimize the following loss function and loss function is calculated as follows:

\begin{algorithm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\caption{Dueling DDQN-based Algorithm for \textbf{FLSO Load Balancing}}\label{alg:2}
    \State \textbf{Initialization:} Random weights $\theta$, replay memory $\mathcal R$ with capacity $\mathcal D$, minibatch size $\mathcal Z$, total episodes $\mathcal E$, prioritization parameter $\alpha$, importance sampling parameter $\beta$, exploration rate $\epsilon$, Polyak averaging parameter $\tau$
    \State \textbf{Input:} State, action, discount factor
    \State \textbf{Output:} Optimal FLSO decision
    \For{$Episode = 1$ to $\mathcal E$}
        \State Initialize the integrated environment
    \For{$t=0$ to $T$}
            \State $a_t = \begin{cases}
            \arg\max_{a'} Q(s_t, a'; \theta) & \text{with } 1 - \epsilon \\
	    	\text{random action} & \text{with } \epsilon
            \end{cases}$
            \State Execute action $a_t$ (select GW $m_t$), observe reward $r_t$ and next state $s_{t+1}$
            \State Store sample $(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})$ into $\mathcal R$
            \If{$\mathcal R > {\mathcal D}$}
                \For{$i=1$ to $\mathcal Z$}
                    \State Sample $(s_i, a_i, r_i, s_{i+1})$ from $\mathcal R$ with probability $p_i^\alpha$
                    \State Calculate target network
                \EndFor
                \State $\theta^- \leftarrow \tau \cdot \theta + (1 - \tau) \cdot \theta^-$
            \EndIf
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

To improve robustness against random noise, such as channel shadow fading, we utilize a dueling DQN \cite{wang2016dueling}. The dueling DQN architecture enhances the traditional DQN by splitting the Q-function into two separate streams: the state-value function \( V(s; \theta) \) and the advantage function \( A(s, a; \theta) \). The Q-function is then calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
& Q(s, a; \theta) = V(s; \theta) + \left( A(s, a; \theta) - \frac{1}{|A|} \sum_{a'} A(s, a'; \theta) \right),
\label{eq:dueling_q}
\end{align}
where \( V(s; \theta) \) represents the value of the state \( s \), \( A(s, a; \theta) \) represents the advantage of taking action \( a \) in state \( s \), and \( |A| \) is the number of possible actions. This decomposition allows the network to better distinguish the value of a state from the advantage of each action, improving robustness against noise and enhancing learning efficiency in complex LEO satellite environments.

To further enhance the learning efficiency, we incorporate PER as proposed in \cite{schaul2015prioritized}. PER prioritizes experiences with higher TD errors, where the TD error \(\delta_i\) for transition \(i\) is defined as the difference between the target and current Q-values. The priority is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
p_i = |\delta_i| + \epsilon_p,
\label{eq:priority}
\end{equation}
where \( \delta_i = r_i + \gamma Q(s_{i+1}, a_{i+1}; \theta^-) - Q(s_i, a_i; \theta) \) is the TD error, and \( \epsilon_p \) ensures non-zero priorities. PER uses \( \alpha \in [0, 1] \) to control sampling probability \(p_i^\alpha \) and \( \beta \in [0, 1] \) to adjust importance sampling weights \((\mathcal D \cdot P(i))^{-\beta} \), where \( \mathcal D \) is replay buffer limit and \(P(i)\) is the probability of sampling transition \(i\). This approach accelerates learning by focusing on significant transitions, which is beneficial in LEO satellite networks with dynamic and noisy conditions.

By combining dueling DQN with DDQN and PER, our approach effectively addresses overestimation bias, accelerates learning, and mitigates the effects of random noise, leading to improved network performance with effective GW load balancing, elevated RSRP, and reduced FLSO frequency.
