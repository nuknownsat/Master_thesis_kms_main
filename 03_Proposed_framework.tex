\section{System model}
\quad This thesis considers the transparent LEO satellite constellation consist of $N$ LEO satellites, represented by the set $\mathcal{S} = \left\{n \mid n = 1, 2, \dots, N\right\}$. The LEO satellite connects to ground GW via feeder link, where the set of GW denoted as $\mathcal{G} = \left\{m \mid m = 1, 2, \dots, M\right\}$. The GWs consists of both an antenna and a gNB. Since the GWs are widely separated, each is equipped with its own dedicated gNB. The coordinates of each LEO satellite in the Earth-centered, Earth-fixed (ECEF) coordinate system at a given time slot $t$, where $t \in \{0, 1, \dots, T\}$, are given by $l_n(t) = (x_n(t), y_n(t), z_n(t))$. As the LEO satellite services the region, it covers UEs, denoted by the set \( U = \{ u_t \mid u_t = N_t(\mu,\sigma^2) \} \), distributed according to a 2-D Gaussian footprint. With a hard FLSO applied, each UE within the LEO satellite’s coverage area simultaneously initiates a HO to the subsequent gNB upon every FLSO. Fig.~\ref{figure_system_model} shows the network scenario where a transparent Starlink LEO satellite passes over $M$ ground GWs in the United States, serving UEs.

The LEO satellite \( n \) evaluates the RSRP from signals transmitted by the GW \( m \) at periodic interval \( T_m \). The RSRP \( R_{m,n} \) is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
R_{m,n} = EIRP_{m} + G_{n} - PL_{m,n} - 10 \log_{10}(q_{RB}),
\label{eq:rsrp}
\end{equation}
where \( EIRP_{m} \) is the effective isotropic radiated power (EIRP) of the GW \( m \), defined as \( EIRP_{m} = P_{m} + G_{m} \), with \( P_{m} \) being the transmit power and \( G_{m} \) the transmit antenna gain of the GW $m$ towards the LEO satellite \( n \). \( G_{n} \) represents the receive antenna gain of the LEO satellite \( n \). The term \( 10 \log_{10}(q_{RB}) \) is used to normalize the RSRP measurement, accounting for a single resource block out of the total \( q_{RB} \) resource blocks allocated to the link. $PL_{m,n}$ is the channel model between the GW \( m \) and the LEO satellite \( n \) is provided in the 3rd generation partnership project (3GPP) documentation \cite{3gppchanel} and is calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
&PL_{m,n} = P_{{LOS,{m,n}}} ( FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m) + SF_{m,n} )\notag\\
&+ P_{{NLOS,{m,n}}} ( FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m) + SF_{m,n} + CL_{m,n} ),
\end{align}
$P_{{LOS,{m,n}}}$ and $P_{{NLOS,{m,n}}}$ denote the probabilities that the LEO satellite \( n \) to GW \( m \) is in a line-of-sight (LOS) and non-LOS (NLOS) condition, respectively. $FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m)$ denotes the free space path loss (FSPL) as a function of the distance $d_{m,n}$ between the LEO satellite \( n \) and the GW \( m \) and the frequency $f_m$ of the GW \( m \) uplink feeder link frequency. $SF_{m,n}$ represents the shadow fading, which accounts for the signal attenuation caused by obstacles such as buildings, trees, or terrain variations in the propagation path. $CL_{m,n}$ denotes the clutter loss, reflecting additional signal degradation due to environmental clutter. Specifically, the $FSPL_{m,n}$ is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
FSPL_{m,n} = 32.45 + 20log_{10}(f_m) + 20log_{10}(d_{m,n}).
\end{equation}

At each measurement instant $t$, the LEO satellite \( n \) samples the RSRP from GW \( m \) and all neighboring GWs within instantaneous visibility region, yielding the raw RSRP $R_{m,n}(t)$. To mitigate the effects of shadow fading and measurement noise, these raw RSRP $R_{m,n}(t)$ samples are processed at the radio resource control (RRC) layer using a layer-3 (L3) infinite impulse response (IIR) filter \cite{3gppl3}. Denoting the filtered RSRP by $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$, the update equation is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t) = a{R}_{m,n}(t) + (1 - a)\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t),
  \label{eq:L3filter_new}
\end{equation}
where $a \in (0,1)$ is the L3 forgetting factor, defined as $a = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{K/4}$, and $K$ is the filter coefficient. The initial condition is set as $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(0) = R_{m,n}(0)$. Finally, the LEO satellite compiles a measurement report containing the L3-filtered RSRP $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$ of the serving and neighboring GWs and transmits this report back to the serving GW \( m \) over the feeder link.

To quantify the link quality during the simulation, we calculate the channel capacity $C$ using the Shannon capacity formula, which is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
C = B_m \log_2(1+{\zeta}),
\label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
where the $B_m$ is the uplink bandwidth of GW \( m \) and $\zeta$ is the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
{\zeta} = {EIRP_{m}}+\frac{G_{n}}{T}-k-{PL_{m,n}}-B_m,
\label{eq:snr}
\end{equation}
where, $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, $G_n/T$ is the antenna gain-to-noise temperature ratio, is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
\frac{G_n}{T} = G_{n} - N_{f} - 10\log_{10}\left(T_0 + (T_a - T_0) 10^{-0.1 N_{f}} \right),
\end{equation}
where $N_{f}$ is LEO satellite noise figure, $T_0$ and $T_a$ is ambient and antenna temperature.



\section{Problem formulation}
In this section, we address the optimization problem of performing optimal FLSO to minimize the number of HO for UE served by a LEO satellite. We formulate this problem as an non-deterministic polynomial time (NP)-hard problem \cite{singlemachine} and propose a solution by modeling it as a Markov decision process (MDP), which is then solved using reinforcement learning techniques.

% The satellite is assumed to connect to a GW only when the elevation angle to that GW exceeds $\theta_{\min}$ and the RSRP surpasses $\sigma_{\min}$, while the GW antenna continuously tracks the LEO satellite. 문제 정의로 ㄱㄱ

In LEO networks, frequent FLSO and HO events cause elevated latency and power consumption. FLSO \(FS_{m,n}\) and HO \(H_{m,n}\) are measured when the serving \( m \) transitions from \(t-1\) to \(t\) calculated as follows:

\begin{equation}
FS_{m,n} = \sum_{t=0}^{T} \left(1 \text{ if } m_t \neq m_{t-1} \text{ else } 0 \right),
\label{eq:flso_cost}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
H_{m,n} = \sum_{t=0}^{T} \left(u_{t-1} \text{ if } m_t \neq m_{t-1} \text{ else } 0 \right).
\label{eq:ho_cost}
\end{equation}
To address these issues, we formulate an optimization problem that enables the LEO satellite to perform optimal FLSO while minimizing UE HO and UFLSO, and maximizing ToS and RSRP, meeting the conditions calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
& \textbf{P1:}\quad \operatorname*{min}_{m} \sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{t=0}^{T} \big( FS_{m,n} + H_{m,n} + UFS_{m,n} \notag \\
& \hspace{8em} - ToS_{m,n} - \widetilde R_{m,n} \big),\\
& \quad \text{s.t.} \nonumber \\
& \quad \textbf{C1 :}\quad \theta_{m,n}\geq \theta_{\text {min}}, \quad \forall{n}, \forall{m}, \forall{t},\\
& \quad \textbf{C2 :}\quad \widetilde R_{m,n} \geq R_{\text {min}}, \quad \forall{n}, \forall{m}, \forall{t},\\
& \quad \textbf{C3 :}\quad I_{m,n}(t) = 1, \quad  \forall{n}, \forall{m}, \forall{t}.
\end{align}

In \textbf{P1}, \( {UFS_{m,n}} \) represents the cost of an UFLSO, which occurs when the LEO satellite \( n \) switches to another GW within the minimum ToS (MTS) and \( {ToS_{m,n}}\) represents ToS the duration of the LEO satellite \( n \) remains connected to the GW \( m \). The objective function minimizes the total cost, which includes the FLSO, HO of UEs and UFLSO events and maximizes ToS and RSRP across \(N\) LEO satellites, \(M\) GWs, and \(T\) time slots. Constraint \textbf{C1} ensures the elevation angle \(\theta_{m,n}\) between LEO satellite \( n \) and GW \( m \) exceeds the minimum threshold \(\theta_{\min}\), guaranteeing reliable communication by maintaining a sufficient line-of-sight. Constraint \textbf{C2} ensures the filtered RSRP \(\widetilde{R}_{m,n}\) exceeds the minimum required rate \(R_{\min}\), ensuring adequate link quality for effective data transmission. Constraint \textbf{C3} enforces that each LEO satellite connects to exactly one GW at all time \(t\), using the indicator variable \(I_{m,n}(t)\), which is 1 if LEO satellite \( n \) is connected to GW \( m \) at time \(t\). 

\section{Proposed algorithm}
\quad To address the problem, we transform it into a dynamic programming framework to derive an optimal FLSO policy. We model the FLSO optimization problem as an MDP due to its ability to handle sequential decision-making in dynamic environments.


\subsection{MDP modeling}
\quad The LEO satellite's state \(s_t\) evolves over time and actions \(a_t\) impact future reward \(r_t\) with discount factor \(\gamma\) \cite{intro_RL}. This framework enables the LEO satellite to learn an optimal FLSO policy through reinforcement learning, effectively balancing service continuity and HO minimization. The state space \(s_t\) of the MDP is calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
s_t &= \left\{ l_n(t), m_t, \widetilde R_{m,n}(t), u_{t} \right\}.
\end{align}
The state is defined as a tuple comprising the LEO satellite's ECEF coordinates \(l_n(t)\), the currently connected GW \( m_t \), the RSRP of the current GW \( \widetilde R_{m,n}(t) \), and the number of UEs currently being served \( u_{t} \) at time $t$, configured to be accessible and interpretable by the LEO satellite.

The action space \( {a_t} \) involves the LEO satellite selecting the optimal GW at each step. Accordingly, our action space is calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
{a_t} &= \{ m_{t} | m_{t} \in \mathcal G_{t} \}.
\end{align}
The action comprises the set of GW \( \mathcal G_{{t}} \) denotes the set of all available GW at $t$. 

The immediate reward \(r_{t}\) is formulated to jointly promote service continuity and signal quality while penalizing both necessary and UFLSO. It is calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
r_t &= (1+ \rho u_{t}){FS}(t) + {UFS}(t) - \Bigl({ToS}(t) + \nu{\widetilde R_{m,n}}(t)\Bigr).
\end{align}
The parameters \(\rho\) and \(\nu\) are normalization factors used to align the values with other metrics.

Discount factor $\gamma$ is a value between 0 and 1, which represents the rate at which future rewards are discounted when considering them at the current step.

\label{Proposed Dueling DQN Algorithm}
\quad In this paper, we employ a reinforcement learning algorithm to enable LEO satellites to operate in an dynamic environment. As depicted in Fig. 2, the architecture of the proposed dueling DQN algorithm, incorporating a \(\epsilon\)-greedy policy, is designed to optimize FLSO in LEO satellite networks. The LEO satellite receives feedback to execute actions that sustain both a prolonged ToS and an elevated RSRP, while minimizing the frequency of FLSO, UFLSO, and UE HO events. We enhance the traditional DQN framework by incorporating double DQN (DDQN), prioritized experience replay (PER), and dueling DQN to improve stability, learning efficiency, and robustness against noise in LEO satellite communication systems.

The DQN algorithm \cite{mnih2013playing} approximates the Q-function \( Q(s_t, a_t; \theta) \), which maps a state \( s_t \) and action \( a_t \) to an expected cumulative reward, using a deep neural network (DNN) parameterized by \( \theta \). Two DNNs are utilized: an online network for action selection and a target network for value estimation. The Q-function is trained to minimize the following loss function and loss function is calculated as follows:
\begin{algorithm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\caption{Dueling DDQN-based Algorithm for UE HO}\label{alg:2}
    \State \textbf{Initialization:} Random weights $\theta$, replay memory $\mathcal R$ with capacity $\mathcal D$, minibatch size $\mathcal Z$, total episodes $\mathcal E$, prioritization parameter $\alpha$, importance sampling parameter $\beta$, exploration rate $\epsilon$, Polyak averaging parameter $\tau$
    \State \textbf{Input:} State, action, discount factor
    \State \textbf{Output:} Optimal FLSO decision
    \For{$Episode = 1$ to $\mathcal E$}
        \State Initialize the integrated environment
    \For{$t=0$ to $T$}
            \State $a_t = \begin{cases}
            \arg\max_{a'} Q(s_t, a'; \theta) & \text{with } 1 - \epsilon \\
	    	ext{random action} & \text{with } \epsilon
            \end{cases}$
            \State Execute action, observe reward and next state
            \State Store sample $(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})$ into $\mathcal R$
            \If{$\mathcal R > {\mathcal D}$}
                \For{$i=1$ to $\mathcal Z$}
                    \State Sample $(s_i, a_i, r_i, s_{i+1})$ from $\mathcal R$ with probability $p_i^\alpha$
                    \State Calculate target network
                \EndFor
                \State $\theta^- \leftarrow \tau \cdot \theta + (1 - \tau) \cdot \theta^-$
            \EndIf
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

To improve robustness against random noise, such as channel shadow fading and variations in UE distribution, we utilize a dueling DQN \cite{wang2016dueling}. The dueling DQN architecture enhances the traditional DQN by splitting the Q-function into two separate streams: the state-value function \( V(s; \theta) \) and the advantage function \( A(s, a; \theta) \). The Q-function is then calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
& Q(s, a; \theta) = V(s; \theta) \quad \notag \\
& + \left( A(s, a; \theta) - \frac{1}{|A|} \sum_{a'} A(s, a'; \theta) \right),
\label{eq:dueling_q}
\end{align}
where \( V(s; \theta) \) represents the value of the state \( s \), \( A(s, a; \theta) \) represents the advantage of taking action \( a \) in state \( s \), and \( |A| \) is the number of possible actions. This decomposition allows the network to better distinguish the value of a state from the advantage of each action, improving robustness against noise and enhancing learning efficiency in complex LEO satellite environments.

To further enhance the learning efficiency, we incorporate PER as proposed in \cite{schaul2015prioritized}. PER prioritizes experiences with higher temporal-difference (TD) errors, where the TD error \(\delta_i\) for transition \(i\) is defined as the difference between the target and current Q-values. The priority is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
p_i = |\delta_i| + \epsilon_p,
\label{eq:priority}
\end{equation}
where \( \delta_i = r_i + \gamma Q(s_{i+1}, a_{i+1}; \theta^-) - Q(s_i, a_i; \theta) \) is the TD error, and \( \epsilon_p \) ensures non-zero priorities. PER uses \( \alpha \in [0, 1] \) to control sampling probability \(p_i^\alpha \) and \( \beta \in [0, 1] \) to adjust importance sampling weights \((\mathcal D \cdot P(i))^{-\beta} \), where \( \mathcal D \) is replay buffer limit and \(P(i)\) is the probability of sampling transition \(i\). This approach accelerates learning by focusing on significant transitions, which is beneficial in LEO satellite networks with dynamic and noisy conditions.

By combining dueling DQN with DDQN and PER, our approach effectively addresses overestimation bias, accelerates learning, and mitigates the effects of random noise, leading to improved LEO satellite HO performance with prolonged ToS, elevated RSRP, and reduced FLSO, UFLSO, and UE HO events.
