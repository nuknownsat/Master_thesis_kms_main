\section{System model}
\quad As in Fig. ~\ref{figure_system_model}, this thesis considers a large-scale LEO satellite constellation deployed across $P$ orbital planes, represented by the set $\mathcal{P} = \left\{p \mid p = 1, \dots, P\right\}$. The constellation consists of $N$ LEO satellites in total, distributed across these planes, with $N_p$ satellites in plane $p$. The overall satellite index set is denoted as $\mathcal{S} = \{ s_{p,i} \mid p = 1, \dots, P;\ i = 1, \dots, N_p \}$, and its cardinality as $\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert = \sum_{p=1}^{P} N_p$. In this proposed framework, the ground GWs are modeled as intelligent agents in a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) setting. The set of agents corresponds to the set of GWs $\mathcal{G} = \{1, \dots, M\}$. Each GW agent $m$ manages its limited number of connection links, denoted as $l_{max}$, to serve multiple LEO satellites simultaneously. The GW monitors the link conditions of connected satellites and visible candidate satellites to make load balancing and FLSO decisions.

To address the primary challenge of GW load balancing, where the instantaneous load $\lvert \mathcal{L}_m(t) \rvert$ is defined by the set of connected satellites, this thesis models the dynamic UE demand of each satellite $n$ as a stochastic time-varying traffic load $\psi_n(t)$. Thereby introducing network uncertainty that necessitates GW agents to adaptively manage connections based on fluctuating aggregate traffic rather than solely on satellite visibility.
% 로드 밸런싱 문제를 해결하기 위해, 각 위성 $n$의 동적 UE 수요를 균등 분포 $\mathcal{U}(0, f)$를 따르는 확률적 시간 가변 트래픽 부하 $\psi_n(t)$로 모델링하여 네트워크 불확실성을 도입하였다. % 이를 통해 지상 GW 에이전트는 단순한 위성 가시성에 의존하는 대신, 변화하는 총 트래픽에 기반하여 적응적으로 연결을 관리해야 한다.

The GW \( m \) receives measurement reports from connected LEO satellites. The satellite \( n \) of GW \( m \) RSRP \( R_{m,n} \) is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
R_{m,n} = EIRP_{m} + G_{n} - PL_{m,n} - 10 \log_{10}(q_{RB}),
\label{eq:rsrp}
\end{equation}
where \( EIRP_{m} \) is the effective isotropic radiated power (EIRP) of the GW \( m \), defined as \( EIRP_{m} = P_{m} + G_{m} \), with \( P_{m} \) being the transmit power and \( G_{m} \) the transmit antenna gain of the GW $m$ towards the LEO satellite \( n \). \( G_{n} \) represents the receive antenna gain of the LEO satellite \( n \). The term \( 10 \log_{10}(q_{RB}) \) is used to normalize the RSRP measurement, accounting for a single resource block out of the total resource blocks \( q_{RB} \) allocated to the link. $PL_{m,n}$ is the large-scale path loss between the GW \( m \) and the LEO satellite \( n \), and this expression is adapted from 3GPP documentation \cite{3gppchanel}. The model is calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
&PL_{m,n} = P_{{LOS,{m,n}}} \big( FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m) + SF_{m,n} \big)\notag\\
&\quad + P_{{NLOS,{m,n}}} \big( FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m) + SF_{m,n} + CL_{m,n}  \big) + AT_{m,n},
\end{align}
where $P_{{LOS,{m,n}}}$ and $P_{{NLOS,{m,n}}}$ denote the probabilities that the LEO satellite \( n \) to GW \( m \) is in a line-of-sight (LoS) and non-line-of-sight (NLoS) condition, respectively. $FSPL_{m,n}(d_{m,n}, f_m)$ denotes the free space path loss (FSPL) as a function of the distance $d_{m,n}$ between the LEO satellite \( n \) and the GW \( m \) and the frequency $f_m$ of the GW \( m \) uplink feeder link frequency. $SF_{m,n}$ represents the shadow fading, which accounts for the signal attenuation caused by obstacles such as buildings, trees, or terrain variations in the propagation path. $CL_{m,n}$ denotes the clutter loss, reflecting additional signal degradation due to environmental clutter. $AT_{m,n}$ captures atmospheric attenuation along the feeder link slant path, including gaseous absorption due to dry air and water vapor, and it can be obtained from standard ITU-R models for the considered band~\cite{ITU-R-P.676-13}. Specifically, the $FSPL_{m,n}$ is calculated as follows:
% 한글 주석: 경로손실 식에 대기 감쇄 AT_{m,n}를 추가하여 LOS/NLOS 모두에 반영합니다. (기체 흡수 등, ITU-R 모델 기반 계산 가능)
\begin{equation}
FSPL_{m,n} = 32.45 + 20\log_{10}(f_m) + 20\log_{10}(d_{m,n}).
\end{equation}

At each measurement instant $t$, the LEO satellite \( n \) samples the RSRP from GW \( m \) and all neighboring GWs within instantaneous visibility region, yielding the raw RSRP $R_{m,n}(t)$. To mitigate the effects of shadow fading and measurement noise, these raw RSRP $R_{m,n}(t)$ samples are processed at the radio resource control (RRC) layer using a layer 3 (L3) infinite impulse response (IIR) filter \cite{rel_38331}. Denoting the filtered RSRP by $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$, the update equation is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t) = a{R}_{m,n}(t) + (1 - a)\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t-1),
  \label{eq:L3filter_new}
\end{equation}
where $a \in (0,1)$ is the L3 forgetting factor, defined as $a = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{K/4}$, and $K$ is the filter coefficient. The initial condition is set as $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(0) = R_{m,n}(0)$. Finally, the LEO satellite compiles a measurement report containing the L3-filtered RSRP $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$ of the serving and neighboring GWs and transmits this report back to the serving GW \( m \) over the feeder link.

\section{Problem formulation}
\quad This section addresses the optimization problem for the LEO satellite constellation. The objective is to find an optimal FLSO policy $\pi$ that dynamically assigns each satellite $n$ to a GW $m$ at each time slot $t$.

The primary goal is to maximize the network-wide utility, which involves minimizing load imbalance and switching instability while maximizing link quality and connection duration. Unlike simple connection counting, this thesis defines the instantaneous traffic load of GW $m$ at time $t$, denoted as $L_m(t)$, as the aggregate traffic demand of all connected satellites:
\begin{equation}
L_m(t) = \sum_{n \in \mathcal{S}_m(t)} \psi_n(t),
\label{eq:gw_load_traffic}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}_m(t)$ denotes the set of connected satellites served by GW $m$ at time $t$ and $\psi_n(t)$ is the dynamic traffic demand of satellite $n$.

To align with the MARL framework, this thesis formulates the problem as a maximization of cumulative rewards. The following reward and penalty components are defined:


\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FLSO Penalty:} A penalty component $C_{FS}$ is incurred if the satellite switches its serving GW, penalizing instability.
    \begin{equation}
    FS_{m,n}(t) = \begin{cases} C_{FS} & \text{if } m_n(t) \neq m_{n}(t-1), \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}
    \label{eq:flso_cost}
    \end{equation}
    where $m_n(t)$ denotes the index of the serving GW connected to satellite $n$ at time $t$.

    \item \textbf{UFLSO Penalty:} A penalty component $C_{UFS}$ is incurred for an unnecessary FLSO (UFLSO), which occurs if the satellite switches to another GW within a minimum time-of-stay (MTS), denoted $\tau_{min}$. Let $\tau_{stay}(t)$ be the time elapsed since the last FLSO.
    \begin{equation}
    UFS_{m,n}(t) = \begin{cases} C_{UFS} & \text{if } m_n(t) \neq m_{n}(t-1) \text{ and } \tau_{stay}(t) < \tau_{min}, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}
    \label{eq:uflso_cost}
    \end{equation}
    
    \item \textbf{ToS Reward:} A reward is gained for maintaining a connection with the same GW, proportional to the duration of the connection.
    \begin{equation}
    ToS_{m,n}(t) = \tau_{stay}(t).
    \label{eq:tos_reward}
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Fairness Reward:} To ensure load balancing based on the stochastic traffic $\psi_n(t)$, a reward is derived from Jain's Fairness Index \cite{jain1984quantitative} applied to the GW traffic loads $L_m(t)$.
    \begin{equation}
    J(t) = \frac{(\sum_{m=1}^{M} L_m(t))^2}{M \cdot \sum_{m=1}^{M} (L_m(t))^2}.
    \label{eq:jain_index_term}
    \end{equation}
    
    \item \textbf{Link Quality Reward:} A reward is gained based on the L3-filtered RSRP from the connected GW to ensure high-quality connectivity.

\end{itemize}

The global optimization problem \textbf{P1} is to find the policy $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ that maximizes the weighted sum of these components over the time horizon $T$:

\begin{align}
& \textbf{P1:}\quad \operatorname*{\max}_{\boldsymbol{\pi}} \mathbb{E} \sum_{t=0}^{T} \Big[ J(t) + \sum_{m=1}^{M} \sum_{n \in \mathcal{S}_m(t)} \Big( \widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t) + ToS_{m,n}(t) - \xi_{m,n}(t) \Big) \Big],\\
& \quad \text{s.t.} \nonumber \\
& \quad \textbf{C1 :}\quad \theta_{m,n}(t) \geq \theta_{\text {min}}, \quad \forall m, \forall n \in \mathcal{S}_m(t), \forall t,\\
& \quad \textbf{C2 :}\quad \widetilde R_{m,n}(t) \geq R_{\text {min}}, \quad \forall m, \forall n \in \mathcal{S}_m(t), \forall t,\\
& \quad \textbf{C3 :}\quad |\mathcal{S}_m(t)| \le l_{\text{max}}, \quad \forall m, \forall t.
\end{align}

In \textbf{P1}, $\xi_{m,n}(t) = FS_{m,n}(t) + UFS_{m,n}(t)$ represents the combined switching penalties. The positive signs for $J(t)$, $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$, and $ToS_{m,n}(t)$ indicate maximization of fairness, link quality, and stability, respectively, while the negative sign for $\xi_{m,n}(t)$ enforces the minimization of switching overheads. Constraint \textbf{C3} ensures that the number of connected satellites does not exceed the GW capacity limit $l_{\text{max}}$.

The constraints define the feasibility conditions for the active connections $\mathcal{S}_m(t)$.
Constraint \textbf{C1} ensures that the elevation angle $\theta_{m,n}(t)$ for every connected link exceeds the minimum threshold $\theta_{\min}$, guaranteeing a reliable LoS.
Constraint \textbf{C2} requires that the L3-filtered RSRP $\widetilde{R}_{m,n}(t)$ of any active connection maintains a minimum signal quality level $R_{\min}$.
Constraint \textbf{C3} imposes a hard capacity limit on the ground segment, ensuring that the number of satellites served by a single GW $m$ does not exceed the maximum link capacity $l_{\text{max}}$.

The FLSO optimization problem involves simultaneous decision-making by multiple satellites, characterized by high-dimensional state spaces and complex, time-varying network topology. This combinatorial nature renders the problem non-deterministic polynomial-time hard (NP-hard).

\section{Proposed method}
\quad To address the problem \textbf{P1}, the optimization problem is formulated as a multi-agent Markov decision process (MMDP)~\cite{ju2023achieving}. This framework allows each GW agent to learn an optimal policy for managing satellite connections through sequential interaction with the dynamic LEO environment.

\subsection{MMDP modeling}
\quad In the proposed framework, each GW $m$ acts as an independent agent. The agent observes its local state $s_{m,t}$, executes an action $a_{m,t}$, and receives a reward $r_{m,t}$. The detailed definitions are as follows:

The state $s_{m,t}$ is designed to provide the agent with comprehensive information about its current utilization and potential handover targets. It consists of two feature vectors:
\begin{align}
    s_{m,t} = \big\{ \mathbf{H}_{m,t}^{links}, \mathbf{H}_{m,t}^{cands} \big\},
\end{align}
where, $\mathbf{H}_{m,t}^{links}$ denotes the status of the agent's $l_{max}$ connection links. For each link $l$, the feature vector contains a binary active feeder link indicator, the normalized RSRP of the connected satellite, the normalized ToS of the current connection, and the satellite's traffic demand. $\mathbf{H}_{m,t}^{cands}$ encodes the top-$K$ visible candidate satellites sorted by signal strength, where each candidate is represented by its normalized RSRP and traffic demand. This compact, unified representation allows the agent to evaluate and compare its current connections against potential alternatives when making connection and FLSO decisions.

The action space $a_{m,t}$ is discrete and defined as the selection of a candidate satellite to serve. At each step $t$, the agent selects an index $p$ from the candidate list:
\begin{align}
    a_{m,t} \in \{0, 1, \dots, p_{cand}\},
\end{align}
where $p_{cand}$ is the number of considered candidates. Selecting a candidate index implies attempting to establish a connection with that satellite. If the candidate is already connected, the action is treated as maintaining the connection. If the candidate is new and the GW has available links, a new connection is formed. If the GW is full, the agent may replace an existing connection based on the policy.

The reward function $r_{m,t}$ is engineered to guide the agent towards maximizing network utility while balancing load and minimizing instability. Based on the objective function in \textbf{P1}, the immediate reward is calculated as:
\begin{align}
    r_{m,t} = & w_r {R}_{m,t} + w_t {T}_{m,t} + w_{c} {C}_{m,t} + w_F J(t) - w_s \mathbb{I}(FLSO_{m,t}) - w_l (L_{m,t})^2,
\end{align}

where, $R_{m,t}$ denotes the average normalized RSRP of satellites connected to GW $m$. $T_{m,t}$ is the average normalized ToS promoting connection stability. $C_{m,t}$ denotes the average traffic throughput served by GW $m$. $J(t)$ is Jain's fairness index defined above. $\mathbb{I}(FLSO_{m,t})$ is an indicator function equal to 1 when an FLSO occurs at GW $m$ at time $t$. $L_{m,t}$ denotes the total traffic load of GW $m$ the squared term imposes a quadratic penalty on load concentration. The weights $w_r, w_t, w_c, w_F, w_s, w_l$ control the relative importance of these objectives, and the discount factor $\gamma\in[0,1]$ determines the emphasis placed on future rewards.

\subsection{Proposed Multi-Agent dueling DDQN algorithm}
%복잡도 측면에서 얼마나 걸리는지 설명, GeForce RTX 3080 Ti GPU에서 수 밀리초 내에 행동을 출력할 수 있다.
\label{Proposed Multi-Agent Dueling DDQN Algorithm}
\quad To solve the formulated MMDP, this thesis proposes a multi-agent dueling DDQN algorithm. Unlike traditional single-agent approaches where satellites make independent FLSO decisions, this thesis framework employs ground GWs as intelligent agents that learn to cooperatively manage traffic loads.

We utilize a parameter sharing strategy where all homogeneous GW agents share a single Q-network parameterized by $\theta$. This approach significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters, accelerates convergence by aggregating experiences from all agents, and ensures scalability regardless of the number of GWs.

As depicted in Fig.\ref{Fig:system_model2}, the architecture incorporates a dueling network structure to separate state-value and advantage estimation, and a DDQN mechanism to mitigate overestimation bias. The shared Q-function $Q(s_{m,t}, a_{m,t}; \theta)$ takes the local state of GW $m$ and its action as input. The loss function is defined as the expectation over experiences sampled from a shared replay buffer:

\begin{equation}
L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(s,a,r,s') \sim \mathcal{R}} \left[ \left( y_t - Q(s, a; \theta) \right)^2 \right],
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where the target value $y_t$ is calculated using the target network $\theta^{-}$:
\begin{equation}
y_t = r + \gamma Q(s', \arg\max_{a'} Q(s', a'; \theta); \theta^{-}).
\label{eq:ddqn_target}
\end{equation}
where \( \theta^- \) represents the parameter of the target network. The target network parameter are updated as \( \theta^- \leftarrow \tau \cdot \theta + (1 - \tau) \cdot \theta^- \), ensuring slow and stable updates to mitigate the moving target problem in the dynamic LEO satellite environment \cite{hazan2019revisiting}.

To improve robustness against random noise, such as channel shadow fading, this thesis utilizes a dueling DQN \cite{wang2016dueling}. The dueling DQN architecture enhances the traditional DQN by splitting the Q-function into two separate streams: the state-value function \( V(s; \theta) \) and the advantage function \( A(s, a; \theta) \). The Q-function is then calculated as follows:
\begin{align}
& Q(s, a; \theta) = V(s; \theta) + \left( A(s, a; \theta) - \frac{1}{|A|} \sum_{a'} A(s, a'; \theta) \right),
\label{eq:dueling_q}
\end{align}
where \( V(s; \theta) \) represents the value of the state \( s \), \( A(s, a; \theta) \) represents the advantage of taking action \( a \) in state \( s \), and \( |A| \) is the number of possible actions. This decomposition allows the network to better distinguish the value of a state from the advantage of each action, improving robustness against noise and enhancing learning efficiency in complex LEO satellite environments.

To further enhance the learning efficiency, this thesis incorporates PER as proposed in \cite{schaul2015prioritized}. PER prioritizes experiences with higher TD errors, where the TD error \(\delta_i\) for transition \(i\) is defined as the difference between the target and current Q-values. The priority is calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
p_i = |\delta_i| + \epsilon_p,
\label{eq:priority}
\end{equation}
where \( \delta_i = r_i + \gamma Q(s_{i+1}, a_{i+1}; \theta^-) - Q(s_i, a_i; \theta) \) is the TD error, and \( \epsilon_p \) ensures non-zero priorities. PER uses \( \alpha \in [0, 1] \) to control sampling probability \(p_i^\alpha \) and \( \beta \in [0, 1] \) to adjust importance sampling weights \((\mathcal D \cdot P(i))^{-\beta} \), where \( \mathcal D \) is replay buffer limit and \(P(i)\) is the probability of sampling transition \(i\). This approach accelerates learning by focusing on significant transitions, which is beneficial in LEO satellite networks with dynamic and noisy conditions.

By combining dueling DQN with DDQN and PER, this approach effectively addresses overestimation bias, accelerates learning, and mitigates the effects of random noise, leading to improved network performance with effective GW load balancing, elevated RSRP, and reduced FLSO frequency. The detailed process of the training is described in Algorithm \ref{alg:dueling-ddqn-flso}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Multi-Agent Dueling DDQN-based FLSO}
\label{alg:dueling-ddqn-flso}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\Require
Replay buffer $\mathcal{D}$, batch size $\mathcal{Z}$, episodes $\mathcal{E}$, discount $\gamma$,
PER parameters $(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon_p)$, exploration $\epsilon$, Polyak $\tau$, learning rate $\eta$

\Ensure
Optimal decentralized policy $\pi$ for GW agents

\State \textbf{Initialize:}
Shared online network $\theta$, target network $\theta^{-}\leftarrow \theta$,
Replay buffer $\mathcal{R}$

\For{$Episode = 1$ to $\mathcal{E}$}
    \State Reset environment, observe initial states $s_{m,0}$ for all $m \in \mathcal{G}$
    \For{$t = 0$ to $T$}
        
        \State \textbf{Decentralized Action Selection:}
        \For{each agent $m$}
            \State $a_{m,t} = \begin{cases}
            \arg\max_{a'} Q(s_{m,t}, a'; \theta) & \text{w.p. } 1 - \epsilon \\
            \text{random action} & \text{w.p. } \epsilon
            \end{cases}$
        \EndFor

        \State Execute joint actions $\mathbf{a}_t$, get rewards $r_{m,t}$ and next states $s_{m,t+1}$
        
        \State \textbf{Store Transitions:}
        \For{each agent $m$}
            \State Store $(s_{m,t}, a_{m,t}, r_{m,t}, s_{m,t+1})$ into $\mathcal{R}$
        \EndFor

        \If{$|\mathcal{R}| \ge \mathcal{Z}$} 
            \State \textbf{Sample minibatch using PER:}
            \For{$i=1$ to $\mathcal{Z}$}
                \State Sample $(s_i,a_i,r_i,s'_{i}) \sim \mathcal{R}$ with $P(i) \propto p_i^\alpha$
                \State $w_i \leftarrow (\mathcal{D}\cdot P(i))^{-\beta}$, normalize $w_i$
                
                \State \textbf{Compute Target \& TD Error:}
                \State $y_i \leftarrow r_i + \gamma\, Q(s'_{i}, \arg\max_{a'} Q(s'_{i},a';\theta);\theta^{-})$
                \State $\delta_i \leftarrow y_i - Q(s_i,a_i;\theta)$
                \State Update priority $p_i \leftarrow |\delta_i| + \epsilon_p$
            \EndFor

            \State \textbf{Update Network:}
            \State $L \leftarrow \dfrac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{Z}} w_i\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{Huber}}(\delta_i)$
            \State $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta L$
            \State $\theta^{-} \leftarrow \tau\,\theta + (1-\tau)\,\theta^{-}$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
\EndFor

\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
