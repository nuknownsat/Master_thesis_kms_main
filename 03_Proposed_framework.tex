\section{System model}
\quad This thesis propose a beam-to-beam HO scenario in an environment with multiple satellites' spot beams in the same constellation, as shown in Fig. \ref{figure_system_model}. The LEO satellite, equipped with several spot beams, operates at a low earth orbit and moves at 7.56 km/s. The HO is considered to occur only between satellites that move in parallel orbits within the same constellation. In the EMC environment, all cells use the same frequency, causing interference. The UE updates orbital information using system information block (SIB) 19 from the satellite and determines its position via global positioning system (GPS).

\section{Problem formulation}
\quad To select an appropriate cell in an EMC environment and address the aforementioned issues, it is essential to define the positional relationship between the UE and the cell. In this thesis, utilize the vertical distance from the cell trajectory to the UE, based on the position information at two instances along the cell trajectory, as illustrated in Fig. \ref{figure_xp_dif}.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1.0\columnwidth]{figure/figure_xp_dif.png}
\caption{Definition of UE's orbital distance ($X_p$)}
\label{figure_xp_dif}
\end{figure}
The $X_p$ is the vertical distance between the cell trajectory and UE, calculated using the coordinates of both the satellite and UE. With the previous coordinates $(x_{t0}, y_{t0})$ and $(x_{t1}, y_{t1})$ provided to the UE through measurements or SIB 19, the trajectory is expressed as a linear equation:

\begin{equation}
(y_{t1} - y_{t0})x - (x_{t1} - x_{t0})y + (x_{t1}y_{t0} - y_{t1}x_{t0}) = 0.
\end{equation}
Using the formula for the foot of a vertical line, we can calculate the vertical distance from the UE coordinates to the cell trajectory line.

\begin{equation}
d = \frac{|Ax_{0} + By_{0} + C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}}.
\end{equation}
Thus, the vertical distance $X_p$ between the UE's position $(x^{ue}_{t1}, y^{ue}_{t1})$ and the cell trajectory line equation is defined as follows:
\begin{equation}
X_p = \frac{|(y_{t1} - y_{t0})x^{ue}_{t1} - (x_{t1} - x_{t0})y^{ue}_{t1} + (x_{t1}y_{t0} - y_{t1}x_{t0})|}{\sqrt{(y_{t1} - y_{t0})^2 + (x_{t1} - x_{t0})^2}}.
\end{equation}
Through this approach, the $X_p$ values for candidate cells can be calculated, thereby establishing the positional relationship between the UE and the cell trajectory in an EMC environment.

\section{Proposed algorithm}
\quad The primary objective of the OID-based CHO is to reduce UHOs that occur in distance-based CHO methods. The proposed method encourages the UE to maintain its connection with the cell in the closest orbit by utilizing the previously defined vertical distance $X_p$. This strategy aims to select a cell that maximizes the Time of Stay (ToS) within the cell, thus providing an ideal scenario to minimize UHOs in the EMC environment.
\begin{figure} [t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\columnwidth]{figure_jtlee/3_i_wanna_longToS.png}
\caption{Ideal scenario for UHO occurrence}
\label{3_i_wanna_longToS}
\end{figure}
As shown in Fig. \ref{3_i_wanna_longToS} (a), selecting the nearest cell in an EMC environment may lead the UE to HO from serving cell A to target cell B, and then back to cell C, which is in the same orbit as cell A. If the ToS in cell B is less than one second, this HO could be considered a UHO \cite{juan_ho_all}. In contrast, as illustrated in Fig. \ref{3_i_wanna_longToS} (b), selecting a cell with the same $X_p$ value as the serving cell follows an ideal scenario where UHOs are prevented as the ToS increases. Additionally, if there is a cell in a closer orbit with a smaller $X_p$ value than the serving cell, it may be more efficient to HO to that cell for better signal strength. Thus, this strategy involves deciding whether to maintain the current orbit or move to a closer orbit, providing flexibility in response to changes in the UE's location based on $X_p$ values.

\subsection{MDP modeling}
\quad The HO triggering condition of the proposed scheme follows the same distance-based entry condition specified in Section \ref{dist_cho_trig}. This mechanism consists of two thresholds, which the UE can obtain from the \textit{distanceThresh} information in SIB 19 \cite{rel_38331} of the satellite. However, the current design, in which the serving satellite manages and transmits individual thresholds for each UE, may result in increased processing overhead. Additionally, conveying these thresholds to the UE solely through SIB 19 is practically limited. An alternative approach is to use Radio Resource Control (RRC) messages, but this also increases signaling overhead and may degrade HO performance.

\begin{figure} [t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1.0\columnwidth]{figure_jtlee/3_definition_of_innercircle.png}
\caption{Definition of inner-circle}
\label{3_defi_inn}
\end{figure}

In this thesis, a common threshold scheme is employed to address challenges associated with individual threshold configurations. This scheme is uniformly applied to all UEs in the serving cell to configure a threshold for triggering HO when approaching the cell edge. The proposed configuration is similar to conventional distance-based CHO mechanisms, but it utilizes cell edge information from both the serving and target cells to determine the thresholds. 
The first threshold, $Thresh_1$, determines whether the UE has exited the serving cell and incorporates the concept of an inner circle. As shown in Fig. \ref{3_defi_inn}, the inner circle utilizes inter-site distance (ISD) to defines the boundary of the serving cell that does not overlap with any candidate cell, and its radius, $d_{rad}^{I}$, is equal to $Thresh_1$. In cases where multiple $d_{rad}^{I}$ values exist, the largest value is defined as the radius of the inner circle. This configuration ensures effective HO when the UE approaches the cell edge. The second threshold, $Thresh_2$, prevents HO from being triggered if the UE reaches the boundary of the serving cell but has not yet entered an appropriate range of the target cell. To achieve this, $Thresh_2$ is set within the outer circle of the target cell. This prevents unnecessary HOs and helps maintain a stable connection.

\begin{equation}
Thresh_1 = d_{rad}^{I} = ISD - d_{rad}^{T}.
\end{equation}

The sizes of the inner and outer circles, which determine the threshold, can be set via parameters such as SIB 19, while the second threshold can be configured differently depending on system requirements. For instance, $Thresh_2$ can be set to trigger HO when the UE enters the valid signal strength range of the target cell, ensuring that the minimum signal strength of the target cell is met.

\subsection{Proposed dueling DQN algorithm}
\quad When the UE meets the preparation event conditions, it sends a MR to the serving cell to initiate the HO procedure. The MR includes the UE's location information and the distance to each neighboring cell $c_i$, denoted as $Ml_{c_i}$. Upon receiving the MR from the UE, the serving cell determines a list of candidate cells. Traditionally, candidate cells are selected based on the MR information provided by the UE. However, in the proposed approach, the serving cell has access to the ephemeris information of the 1-tier cells, and thus can include all the surrounding cells of the UE as candidate cells. Additionally, neighboring cells that do not exceed the maximum candidate cell threshold $d_{max}^c$, based on the UE's location and $Ml_{c_i}$ obtained from the MR, can also be included as candidate cells. Where, $d_{max}^c$ represents the maximum distance considered for the surrounding cells, which can be determined as, for example, the maximum ISD that can be included as a 1-tier cell.
% Additionally, neighboring cells that do not exceed the maximum candidate cell threshold $d_{max}^c$, based on the UE's location and $Ml_{c_i}$ obtained from the MR, can also be included as candidate cells.

\begin{equation}
C_{candi} = \{ c_i \in C \mid Ml_{c_i} \leq d_{max}^c \}.
\end{equation}
The candidate cell set is defined as $C_{candi} = {c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n}$. The proposed full OID-based CHO procedure is illustrated in Fig. \ref{3_oid_procedure}, where the candidate cell selection described earlier corresponds to step 3, cell filtering. The subsequent procedures are identical to the CHO preparation phase in Section 2.2.2.

\quad After receiving the HO command, the UE waits to verify the execution event. Upon satisfying the execution event, the UE executes an algorithm to select the target cell, $C_t$. This algorithm, based on the $X_p$ values of the serving and candidate cells, is designed to prevent UHOs and ensure that the UE meets the HO conditions with the $C_t$.

First, for each cell $c_n$ in $C_{candi}$, an $X_p^{c_n}$ value is calculated based on the UE’s location. Similarly, the $X_p$ value of the current serving cell, denoted as $C_s$, is represented as $X_p^{C_s}$, where $C_s$ refers to the cell to which the UE is currently connected. To filter valid candidate cells for selection, the $X_p$ values of candidate cells are compared with $X_p^{C_s}$:

\begin{equation}
C_{valid} = \{ c_n \in C_{candi} \mid X_p^{c_n} \leq X_p^{C_s} \}.
\end{equation}

If $C_{valid}$ is formed, the UE selects the cell with the smallest $X_p$ value in the $C_{valid}$ set as the target cell $C_t$. However, if $C_{valid}$ is empty, the UE waits until the next execution event is satisfied and then re-executes this process. To avoid indefinite waiting, a fallback mechanism is implemented. When the value of $Ml_s$ exceeds a certain maximum threshold ($d_{max}^{c_s}$), a fallback mechanism is triggered. For example, it can mean the distance at which the signal quality degrades below a predefined threshold. In such a case, the closest cell in the set $C_{candi}$ is selected as $C_{t}$. This fallback mechanism is consistent with the conventional distance-based CHO scheme. Therefore, the overall $C_t$ selection algorithm in the execution phase is structured as follows:

\begin{equation}
C_t =
\begin{cases} 
\arg\min_{c_v \in C_{valid}} X_p^{c_v}, & \text{if } C_{valid} \neq \emptyset, \\[10pt]
\arg\min_{c_n \in C_{candi}} Ml_n, & \text{if } C_{valid} = \emptyset \text{ and } Ml_s \geq d_{max}^{c_s}, \\[10pt]
\text{Re-evaluate $C_{valid}$}, & \text{if } C_{valid} = \emptyset \text{ and } Ml_s < d_{max}^{c_s}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

This proposed algorithm effectively selects the optimal target cell for the UE in EMC environments by leveraging satellite orbital information and the UE’s location. These inputs, already collected during distance-based CHO procedures, enable precise target cell selection with minimal processing overhead or additional signaling requirements. The pseudocode of this algorithm is detailed in Algorithm \ref{oid_select_algorithm}.

The proposed method is highly effective for selecting the optimal cell for the UE in an EMC environment. By utilizing satellite orbit information and the UE’s location, which are already collected during distance-based CHO, the relative position to the orbit can be considered without additional information from the UE, enabling this process with minimal processing load or computational overhead.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{OID-based CHO: Target Cell Selection}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} $C_{\text{candi}} = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_n\}$ (Candidate cells), $C_s$ (Serving cell), $X_p^{C_s}$ (orbital distance of $C_s$)
\State \textbf{Output:} $C_t$ (Target cell for HO) or \texttt{None}

\If{$C_{\text{candi}} = \emptyset$}
    \State \textbf{return} \texttt{None} \Comment{No candidate cells available}
\EndIf

\If{$Ml_s \geq d_{max}^{c_s}$}
    \State $C_t \gets \arg \min_{c_n \in C_{\text{candi}}} Ml_n$
    \State \textbf{return} $C_t$
\EndIf

\If{Execution event is satisfied}
    \State Construct $C_{\text{valid}} \gets \{c_n \in C_{\text{candi}} \mid X_p^{c_n} \leq X_p^{C_s}\}$
    \If{$C_{\text{valid}} = \emptyset$}
        \State \textbf{return} \texttt{None} \Comment{No valid cells, exit and retry execution event}
    \Else
        \State $C_t \gets \arg \min_{c_v \in C_{\text{valid}}} X_p^{c_v}$
        \State \textbf{return} $C_t$
    \EndIf
\EndIf

\State \textbf{return} \texttt{None} \Comment{No valid cells selected after all conditions}
\end{algorithmic}
\label{oid_select_algorithm}
\end{algorithm}
